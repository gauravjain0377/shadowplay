Open the Cockpit

Ad ops briefs arrive as chaos.

Emails are forwarded three times before anyone acts on them. Budget is "around $10k-ish." Regions are spelled differently in every message — "US," "United States," "USA," "North America." Someone has to map all of this to CPM rates, ad formats, and inventory IDs by hand.

When that mapping is wrong, the campaign launches with the wrong budget, the wrong format, or oversold inventory. The mistake costs money and takes a week to unwind. ShadowPlay does the mapping automatically — and refuses to touch anything until a human signs off.

Five sectors. One ghost lap.

Every brief runs through the same five steps in sequence. A single token traces the path. If a step fails, it retries — just that segment, not the whole lap.

Step Time What it does
Extract 180 ms Parses the raw email into structured fields: brand, budget, regions, flight dates, and audience.
Retrieve 45 ms Fetches matching inventory from the local catalog based on region and format constraints.
Plan 320 ms Generates a ranked media plan with spend allocation, CPM, and attention quality scores per line item.
Verify 90 ms Runs all 7 deterministic guardrail checks — inventory, budget, impressions, regions, CPM floor, and attention.
Log 15 ms Writes the full run record (input, plan, verification result, timestamp) to a local JSON log.

These timings are from a real offline run. Live LLM calls take 2–8 s.

All checks are deterministic — no LLM trust.

The ghost car never crashes the race.

In a racing game, the ghost car replays a previous lap perfectly — same line, same braking points, exact timing — without touching anything on the track. It cannot interfere. It just runs.

ShadowPlay works the same way. It reads the brief, runs the full pipeline, verifies the plan, and stops. No booking is made. No budget is committed. The agent produces a recommendation; a human makes the call.

This is how you introduce AI to a live workflow without risk. Every approval and rejection is tracked. The approval rate becomes a signal — when it's high enough and consistent enough, you can consider expanding scope. Until then, the ghost laps keep running.
